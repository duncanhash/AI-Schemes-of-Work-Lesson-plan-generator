2. SPECIFIC LEARNING OUTCOMES

  1. Identify the biotic and abiotic components of a local ecosystem.
  2. Explain the various types of interactions among organisms in an ecosystem.
  3. Appreciate the interdependence of living and non-living things in maintaining ecological balance.

3. KEY INQUIRY QUESTIONS

  1. How do living and non-living components interact in our school environment?
  2. What would happen to the organisms in an ecosystem if one component were removed?
  3. Why is it important to conserve the balance of interactions within an ecosystem?

4. CORE COMPETENCIES & VALUES

  • Communication: Learners will discuss and present their observations and findings.
  • Critical Thinking: Learners will analyze the relationships between different components of an ecosystem.
  • Creativity: Learners will devise ways to represent ecosystem interactions.
  • Collaboration: Learners will work in groups to observe and collect data about their local environment.
  • Citizenship: Learners will appreciate the importance of environmental conservation and responsible interaction with their surroundings.

5. PCIs

  • Environmental Education: Understanding the delicate balance of ecosystems and the impact of human activities.
  • Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs): Goal 15 (Life on Land) through promoting responsible use of terrestrial ecosystems.
  • Climate Change: Discussing how disruptions in ecosystem interactions can contribute to or be affected by climate change.

6. LEARNING RESOURCES

  • Integrated Science Learner's Book Grade 8 (KICD curriculum).
  • Charts depicting various ecosystems (e.g., forest, pond).
  • Locally available materials: samples of soil, leaves, small stones, insects (if safely observable), water, potted plants from the school compound.
  • Hand lenses/magnifying glasses.
  • Worksheets for recording observations.

9. FACILITATOR'S REFLECTION

What went well? What needs improvement? Follow-up action?
Learners were highly engaged during the outdoor observation activity. The use of hand lenses sparked great curiosity. Group discussions were productive, and many learners grasped the concept of interdependence quickly. Some learners found it challenging to articulate the less obvious interactions (e.g., decomposition, nutrient cycling) without more direct prompting. Time management for group presentations could be improved. Next lesson, provide more structured prompts or a diagram to explain complex interactions. Allocate specific time limits for presentations and use a timer. Consider a pre-recorded video or animated explanation for abstract concepts like nutrient cycling.
